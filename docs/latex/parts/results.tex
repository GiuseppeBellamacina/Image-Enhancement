\section{Experimental Results}
\label{sec:results}

All experiments were conducted on DIV2K~\cite{div2k} with Gaussian noise $\sigma = 100$. Models were trained on 800 training images and evaluated on 10 validation images (indices 0801--0810) at full resolution using sliding-window inference with Hann blending.

\textbf{Key observations:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item NAFNet-V2 achieves the best overall performance across all metrics, benefiting from its multi-stage training pipeline and Charbonnier loss.
    \item Pix2Pix with residual learning ranks second, demonstrating that the adversarial component acts as an effective perceptual regularizer when combined with dominant reconstruction losses.
    \item The top 4 models (NAFNet-V2, Pix2Pix, RIDNet, DnCNN) all employ residual learning, confirming its importance for heavy noise removal.
    \item The Attention UNet with bottleneck attention and GroupNorm outperforms the base UNet by +0.23~dB, demonstrating the benefit of selective feature weighting.
    \item The Denoising Autoencoder, despite lacking skip connections, achieves competitive SSIM (0.660), suggesting the bottleneck forces learning of global structure.
    \item UNet Residual underperforms the direct-prediction UNet, likely due to early stopping (only 10 epochs) preventing convergence.
\end{itemize}

The DnCNN ablation (Table~\ref{tab:dncnn_ablation}) reveals three key findings: (1) A smaller network (17 layers, 64 features) outperforms the larger one (20 layers, 128 features), suggesting that over-parameterization hurts for this noise level. (2) Zero weight decay is critical---any regularization harms the residual learning capacity. (3) Learning rate is the single most impactful factor: reducing LR from $10^{-3}$ to $10^{-4}$ drops PSNR by 2.74~dB.

Gradient clipping (norm = 0.5) proved essential for training stability with larger EAB counts.

The Attention UNet ablation (Table~\ref{tab:attunet_ablation}) reveals a surprising finding: applying attention gates at \textit{all} decoder levels actually \textit{hurts} performance compared to bottleneck-only attention. This suggests that excessive attention modulation in earlier decoder stages can disrupt low-level feature propagation.

\subsubsection{Pix2Pix: Residual Learning and GAN Stabilization}

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\small
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{2.5pt}
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\toprule
\textbf{Variant} & \textbf{$\lambda_{L1}$} & \textbf{LR$_D$} & \textbf{PSNR} & \textbf{SSIM} \\
\midrule
Symmetric LR & 100 & 2e-4 & 23.77 & 0.584 \\
$\lambda_{L1}$ = 1000 & 1000 & 5e-5 & 25.27 & 0.683 \\
Asym.\ LR (standard) & 200 & 1e-5 & 25.65 & 0.720 \\
\textbf{Residual + no Tanh} & \textbf{200} & \textbf{1e-5} & \textbf{26.08} & \textbf{0.733} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Pix2Pix ablation on GAN stabilization strategies. Residual learning with unconstrained output (no Tanh) achieves best results by reducing the generator's learning burden.}
\label{tab:pix2pix_ablation}
\end{table}

The Pix2Pix ablation (Table~\ref{tab:pix2pix_ablation}) reveals critical factors for GAN-based denoising: (1) Symmetric learning rates cause discriminator dominance and training collapse. (2) Asymmetric LR ($\text{LR}_D = 10^{-5}$ vs.\ $\text{LR}_G = 2 \times 10^{-4}$) stabilizes training by slowing the critic. (3) Enabling \textbf{residual learning} and removing Tanh provides the decisive improvement (+0.43~dB over non-residual), as the generator only needs to estimate high-frequency noise rather than reconstruct full images. This transforms Pix2Pix into a residual UNet with adversarial regularization.

\subsubsection{NAFNet-V2: Multi-Stage Training Pipeline}

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\small
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{2.5pt}
\begin{tabular}{lccccc}
\toprule
\textbf{Stage} & \textbf{Loss} & \textbf{LR} & \textbf{Epochs} & \textbf{PSNR} & \textbf{SSIM} \\
\midrule
Base (L1 only) & L1 & 1e-3 & 32 & 26.01 & 0.726 \\
Base (Charbonnier) & Charb. & 1e-3 & 32 & 26.29 & 0.738 \\
Fork (1 patch/img) & Charb. & 1e-3 & +80 & 26.36 & 0.746 \\
\textbf{Fine-tune} & \textbf{Charb.+SSIM} & \textbf{2e-5} & \textbf{+16} & \textbf{26.36} & \textbf{0.747} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{NAFNet-V2 progressive training pipeline. Charbonnier loss provides +0.28~dB over L1; forking with reduced patches enables longer training; final fine-tuning stabilizes SSIM.}
\label{tab:nafnet_ablation}
\end{table}

The NAFNet-V2 ablation (Table~\ref{tab:nafnet_ablation}) demonstrates the value of staged training: (1) Replacing L1 with Charbonnier loss provides +0.28~dB by eliminating gradient discontinuity at zero. (2) Forking from a pre-trained checkpoint with reduced patches per image (4$\to$1) allows 80 additional epochs at lower per-epoch cost. (3) Fine-tuning at very low LR ($2 \times 10^{-5}$) with a small SSIM component ($\beta = 0.05$) stabilizes final weights, reaching peak SSIM of 0.747. Note that a failed fine-tuning attempt with LR $= 10^{-4}$ degraded PSNR to 25.71~dB, confirming that late-stage optimization requires extremely conservative learning rates.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{NAFNet-V2 benefits from staged training:} The multi-stage pipeline allows aggressive initial learning (LR=$10^{-3}$) followed by conservative fine-tuning (LR=$2 \times 10^{-5}$), effectively exploring the loss landscape at multiple scales.
    \item \textbf{GAN training exhibits characteristic oscillation:} Pix2Pix loss curves show adversarial dynamics; despite oscillation, the generator steadily improves thanks to the dominant reconstruction loss ($\lambda_{L1} = 200$).

    \item \textbf{RIDNet combines fast convergence with high quality:} Despite its depth (8 EAB blocks), gradient clipping and cosine scheduling enable smooth convergence in 44 epochs.
    \item \textbf{Learning rate is the dominant factor for DnCNN:} The successful run (LR=$10^{-3}$) converges steadily, while the failed run (LR=$10^{-4}$) plateaus early and runs for 10+ hours without meaningful improvement.
\end{itemize}
