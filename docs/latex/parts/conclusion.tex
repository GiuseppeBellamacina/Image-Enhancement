\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This work presented a comprehensive comparative study of seven CNN-based architectures for image denoising under severe Gaussian noise ($\sigma = 100$). All models were implemented within a unified, modular framework and evaluated under identical conditions on the DIV2K dataset.

\subsection{Key Findings}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{RIDNet achieves state-of-the-art performance} within our comparison (PSNR = 25.83~dB, SSIM = 0.725), demonstrating that the combination of dilated convolutions for multi-scale feature capture and channel attention for adaptive feature weighting is highly effective for heavy noise removal.
    
    \item \textbf{Residual learning is consistently beneficial} for sequential architectures (DnCNN, RIDNet), while encoder-decoder architectures show mixed results---the UNet with residual learning underperformed its direct-prediction counterpart due to insufficient training.
    
    \item \textbf{Attention mechanisms require careful design:} Bottleneck-only attention with GroupNorm outperforms all-level attention with BatchNorm by +2.36~dB, suggesting that over-application of attention gates can disrupt feature propagation in skip connections.
    
    \item \textbf{Upsampling strategy matters for structured degradations:} Learned transposed convolutions outperform bilinear upsampling on dithering (+1.21~dB) but show minimal difference on Gaussian noise, indicating that upsampling choice should be degradation-aware.
    
    \item \textbf{Training hyperparameters can dominate architecture choice:} A 10$\times$ learning rate reduction degrades DnCNN by 2.74~dB---more than the gap between the best and worst architectures. Gradient clipping (norm = 0.5) proved essential for RIDNet stability.
    
    \item \textbf{Skip connections dramatically accelerate convergence:} UNet variants converge in 10--36 epochs vs.\ 200 epochs for the skip-free Denoising Autoencoder, highlighting the importance of direct gradient paths for efficient training.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Architectural Insights}

The results suggest a hierarchy of architectural components by importance:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textit{Learning paradigm} (residual vs.\ direct) --- largest impact for sequential networks;
    \item \textit{Multi-scale feature capture} (dilated convolutions, large receptive fields) --- enables capturing noise at different frequencies;
    \item \textit{Channel attention} (SE blocks) --- provides adaptive feature selection;
    \item \textit{Skip connections} --- critical for training efficiency and high-frequency detail preservation;
    \item \textit{Upsampling strategy} --- secondary for Gaussian noise but important for structured degradations.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Future Directions}

Potential extensions include: (1) evaluating on real-world noise distributions beyond synthetic Gaussian noise; (2) exploring ensemble methods combining the complementary strengths of RIDNet and DnCNN observed in per-image analysis; (3) investigating self-supervised denoising approaches that do not require clean reference images; and (4) extending the framework to other restoration tasks (super-resolution, deblurring, JPEG artifact removal).
